# 3 · Voices and video elements

**Purpose.** One picked voice per character, saved as a voice Higgsfield can reuse; the element line, cut from the picked candidate, and every film line spoken in the voice as references; and one 5 s split-screen video element per speaking character that carries face, costume and voice into every scene.

## Inputs
- The locked head-to-toe sheet of every character and its approved wardrobe pick ([[Flow v3.2 - 2 Characters]]).
- For the voice pick: the character's picked face (`cast/C0N-<name>/pick-face.png`) and the lines that say who the character is, from the face card ([[Flow v3.2 - 2 Characters]], step 11).
- The cast's line list from the locked script `$FILM/script.md` ([[Flow v3.2 - 1 Setup and story]]), written into `$FILM/voices/spec.py` (copied from `tools/voices/spec_template.py`): `TITLE`, `SPEC` (brief, instruction and candidate text per character), `LINES` (id, character, tags, parts), `GAP`, `BEAT`, `GAP_BEFORE`, `TEMPO`, `EQUIV`, `ALLOW_EVENTS`.
- `$FILM/higgsfield.json` (the film's Higgsfield project and folder ids) and `$FILM/elements.json`, both made at setup ([[Flow v3.2 - 1 Setup and story]]).
- Standing rules: [[Flow v3.2 - 0 Standing rules]]

## Steps

**Voice candidates**

Higgsfield has no voice design from a description. A character's candidates come from three places: the preset voices that fit, a voice made from the picked face, and variants of the closest preset. Every paid voice job here goes through the `higgsfield` MCP server; the scripts handle the files before and after.

1. Write one brief per character in `SPEC`. One voice per character, never a real person's voice.
   - Write each brief from the character's picked face.
   - Age, accent, manner and pitch in words ("a very deep, low bass voice, well below average pitch").
   - Loudness: "speaks up clearly, never whispering". Never write "breathy", "close-mic" or "never raising her voice": they make the voice whisper. A voice that must carry a room: "projected, carries across a large room, never breathy".
   - Make a loud character loud in the voice itself. Never push a quiet voice with loud tags: the tags lift the pitch far above it.
   - No era words for the voice ("1940s screen siren"): they give a tinny old-radio timbre that EQ does not remove. Put an era in as acting ("an actor in a British film from the 1980s: a little natural grain") together with "a full, present voice: not tinny, not boxy, not like an old radio".
   - From the brief, write the character's `instruction` for the variants: at most 128 characters, a few words each for pitch, manner, accent and loudness ("low, dry and calm; clear British English; speaks up, never breathy").
   - Candidate text: the element line first, "This is how I look and this is how I sound.", then the character's own calm lines, at least 12 s of speech in all. Every generated candidate speaks it, so the options are measured on the same calm line, the picked one carries the element line, and it is long enough to be saved as a voice (step 5 needs 10 s or more).
2. Run a round: three kinds of candidate, made side by side. Save every file as `voices/options/<CID>-<name>-<round>-<n>.mp3` without echoing its link, and record each with `hf_candidates.py add` (Tools), which measures its pitch and length and writes the round into `voices/options.json` in the shape the app reads.
   - **Preset voices** (free). Call `list_voices` and page through `next_cursor` to the end. Each preset has a `voice_id`, a `name`, a `gender` and a `preview_url`. Keep those of the character's gender, download each preview, measure its pitch (`measure.py`), and keep up to three whose pitch fits the brief. The names say nothing of age or accent: the user's ear decides on the card.
   - **A voice from the face.** `generate_audio` with `model: seed_audio`, the picked face (uploaded with `upload.py`) as the one `image_references` item, no voice and no audio reference (the three are exclusive), the candidate text as the prompt, `format: mp3`, `sample_rate: 44100`, `folder_id` the `cast` folder of `$FILM/higgsfield.json`. One job, one candidate. How well it reads age and type from the face is untested: say so on the card.
   - **Variants of a preset.** `generate_audio` with `model: qwen_audio_tts`, `voice_type: preset`, `voice_id` the preset that fits best, the character's `instruction`, `language: en`, `batch_size: 2`, the candidate text as the prompt, the same `folder_id`. One call, two candidates.
   - Cost: the presets are free; a voice from the face is about 0.3 to 1.1 credits by the length of the text; a variant call is about 0.02 credits. The script lock covers the round: say its size and cost in one line and log every paid job. Run no extra paid rounds and no A/B takes unless the user asks.
3. Voices wait for the face: make a character's candidates only after that character's face is picked, from the brief written from the face, never at the script lock. Head the voice card with the picked face and the same lines that say who the character is as on the face card. Give each option its kind (preset, from the face, variant of which preset) and its measured pitch, beside the voices already locked. Put the round on the Voices page as a card that asks (a pick), as the app's show recipe says.
   - A preset's preview speaks its own text, not the character's: say so on the card. Its pitch is measured on that text.
   - Every voice card also lists the film's voice candidates that were not picked, the other characters' included. The user may pick one of them for this character.
   - Every pick also goes into the library, the Hitchcock Harness's own (Faces, Voices, Characters and Locations in its sidebar), which holds the picks of every film; at every voice pick the library's voices are a source beside the round ([[Flow v3.2 - 0 Standing rules#Showing the work and judging]]).
   - "Deeper" means a measured lower median pitch: put the word-exact option with the lowest pitch first, never the loudest.
   - Compare the new voice with the rest of the cast on calm speech, never on dramatic lines (they sit far higher). Two voices of the same register and accent close in pitch sound the same: when two sit close, show both with their pitches and run a new round for the one the user names.
4. **GATE: voice pick.** Wait for the user's pick on the voice card, by option number (`<CID>` option N). Nothing is saved until the pick.
5. Save the pick: `pick.py <CID> <N> --note '<the user's words, the date>'` (a press on the card runs it). It writes `voices/cast.json` and the chosen option in `voices/options.json`. Speak no sample lines. Then make the voice reusable:
   - A preset is reusable as it is (`voice_type: preset`). Nothing to make.
   - A voice from the face or a variant exists only as its sample. Upload the candidate's file (`upload.py plan`, `media_upload`, `upload.py put`, `media_confirm` with `type: audio`), then call `create_voice_from_confirmed_audio` with that `audio_media_id` and `name: <TITLE> – <name>`. It needs 10 s to 3 minutes of clean speech. It is charged on success and cannot be preflighted; it is part of the round the lock covers. While note 13 has no price for it, read `balance` before and after the first one and write the measured price there; log each in `spend.md`. A new voice starts as `processing`: check it with `list_voices` until it is ready before anything speaks in it. Then record it: `pick.py <CID> --voice-id <id> --voice-type element --source '<kind> option N, saved from its sample'`.
   - Never call `create_voice`: it opens a recording widget.
6. The film's lines are spoken in the saved voice on `elevenlabs_v4` (step 10). A saved voice may sound different from its sample, and that is fine: do not compare the lines with the candidate. The user hears the first lines on the Voices page (step 10); go back to step 2 only on the user's word.
   - A candidate right for its first seconds that drifts later: save the voice from its good part only, 10 s or more, cut with ffmpeg (step 5 on the cut).
   - Never offer speech-to-speech of the user's own read as a fix. Their read is direction for the tags.

**The element line**
7. Cut the element line, "This is how I look and this is how I sound." (about 3.3 s), from the picked candidate itself (`voices/options/<CID>-<name>-<round>-<n>.mp3`, the file of the chosen option in `voices/options.json`), exactly as it sounds: no new take, no tags, no speed change, no clean-up. A picked preset's preview does not speak the line: speak it once in that preset on `elevenlabs_v4` (one turn, the line alone), save it as `voices/options/<CID>-<name>-element-take.mp3`, and cut from that take. First take the file's word times by Whisper on the Mac, free, into a `.stt.json` beside it (`tools/stt/transcribe.py --audio <file> --words-out <the file's name>.stt.json`, `tools/stt/README.md`), then run `tools/voices/element_line.py <CID>` (`--dry-run` shows the cut first; it never replaces an existing cut without `--force`, and then keeps the old pair beside it). It does this:
   - Read the word times. Cut from 0.1 s before "This" to 0.35 s after "sound", or to the middle of the pause when the next word starts sooner, with a 40 ms fade at each end (`film.trim`'s room).
   - Save the cut as `cast/<CID>-<name>/voice/element-line-5s.mp3` and a copy of the whole candidate as `element-line-5s-raw.mp3` beside it. Check the cut's words (word for word) and measure it with `measure.py`. List both in `voices/element-lines-5s.json` (name, option, voice_id, voice_type, note, raw, file, seconds_raw, seconds, transcript, f0).
8. Put each element line on the Voices page, on a card of its own that plays only the element line and asks for a yes.
9. **GATE: element line.** Wait for the user's yes on each element line's card, by ID (`<CID>` element line), before any element is generated: video is the expensive step, so every input is final first.
   - The same recording is the audio reference for a character who is heard but not seen.

**Speaking the film's lines on `elevenlabs_v4`**
10. Speak every line in `LINES` on Higgsfield: `generate_audio` (`generate_audio_batch` for many lines at once) with `model: elevenlabs_v4`, `dialogue` one turn `{text, voice_type, voice_id}` with the voice from `voices/cast.json`, `folder_id` the `cast` folder. Save each result as `audio/raw/<VID>-<name>-<n>.mp3` without echoing its link, where `<n>` is the part number: 1 for a line with no (beat), 1, 2… for the parts of a line split at its (beat)s. A new take of a part is saved as a newer file under the same name pattern; `record.py` uses the newest file of each part. `record.py --todo` lists the takes still to speak, with their text. Then run `record.py`: it takes the new files from `audio/raw/`, checks each by its transcript (the Whisper route), keeps the raw take, trims it, applies `TEMPO`, and builds `audio/lines.json` and the table read.
    - There is no `previous_text` on Higgsfield. Where a line needs the one before (a reply, a one-word line), send the line before as the turn before it in the same `dialogue`, in its own speaker's voice, and `record.py` cuts the line out by its word times. A one-word line spoken alone repeats itself.
    - There is no language setting: put the first lines of each voice on the Voices page before speaking the rest, so the user hears the accent.
    - Tags go in the text in brackets: a few words, one idea each ("[dry, deadpan]"), never a sentence: phrase tags get read aloud, and long sentence-style directions make the model say the line three times. Check the first take that carries a tag.
    - A change of manner halfway is a second short bracket inside the same take ("[warm] Come in. [suddenly wary] Who sent you?"), never two spliced takes. CAPS mark a stressed word.
    - Excited or bright tags raise the pitch. Retake calmer ("a touch brighter, warmer, still low and calm").
    - No sigh in a recording Seedance will hear: put the sigh in the video prompt as an action, or Seedance holds about 2 s of breath before the words.
    - A written (beat) is a known silence: speak the line in parts and let `record.py` join them with a set silence (`BEAT`), so the pause exists in the file, and say in the scene prompt to keep it.
11. Pace: `elevenlabs_v4` on Higgsfield has no speed setting. Set the film's `TEMPO` in `spec.py` before the step 10 run (one factor, or one per character); `record.py` speeds each take with ffmpeg `atempo`, pitch kept. A pace change after recording: `speed_up.py --all --factor <f>`, and set the same `TEMPO` so lines recorded later match.
12. Stress: the user hears where it falls. Measure per-word level and pitch with `emphasis.py <VID> --word <word>` (the stressed word can be the highest rather than the loudest), then make two or three takes on `elevenlabs_v4` (CAPS on the word, or a short non-verbal; `batch_size` up to 4 for variations of one text), save them as `audio/takes/<VID>-<X>.mp3` and list them with `takes.py <VID> --add A <file> --add B <file>`. Put them on a card that asks (a pick). **GATE:** wait for the user's pick by take letter; record it with `takes.py <VID> --choose <X> --note '...'`.
13. A line that has to fit a time is made faster first: `speed_up.py <VID> --factor 1.1` up to 1.45, or `--target <s>`, on the approved take, re-checked by transcript. Grow the time only if the delivery would suffer.
14. A new or changed line: speak it in the character's voice (step 10) and time it from its word times before its generation or previz changes. How a generation's length follows its lines: [[Flow v3.2 - 5 Previz and storyboards]].
15. Pad: Seedance refuses audio references under 2 s (a 422). Run `pad_short.py` after recording and again after every speed-up or edit: a line under 2 s gets trailing silence to 2.6 s.
16. Keep recordings raw. Every raw take stays in `audio/raw/` before any trim, never as the only copy in a scratch folder. Trim with room: 0.1 s before the first word, 0.35 s after the last, a 40 ms fade at each end (`film.trim`).
17. Write every pick down with the user's words and the date (`chosen_take`, `note`, `alternates`), and carry them over on every re-run: a re-run that rebuilds the file wipes the picks.
18. The recordings are references. The film keeps each clip's own sound ([[Flow v3.2 - 9 Edit]]); a recording goes into the cut only to fix a word (below) or when the user asks.

**The video element**
19. Make one element per speaking character. Extras get none: they go into scenes as their group picture, cropped to each scene ([[Flow v3.2 - 2 Characters]], step 8).
   - A character with a second look (other clothes, some years older) keeps the one element. A scene with the second look gets that element plus the second look's sheet ([[Flow v3.2 - 6 Scene prompts]]). Make a separate element for the second look only when the first look's clothes leak into it. A voice that comes out off does not call for a new element.
20. Write the element prompt from this template and save it as `elements/<CID>/E-<CID>-v<N>-5s.prompt.txt` (N: the prompt version). Fill "Preserve exactly" with every face, hair, skin and costume detail on the sheet.
    ```
    Animate the attached approved character sheet of <NAME> into a 5-second video character reference: a SPLIT SCREEN for the whole clip, two halves side by side, a plain seamless mid-grey studio background in both, even neutral studio light, the camera still, no camera orbit.

    RIGHT HALF, the whole 5 seconds: a steady head-and-shoulders portrait of <NAME>, three-quarter to camera, looking straight into the lens and speaking the line in the attached voice recording, word for word, in that exact voice: "This is how I look and this is how I sound." <an expression on a named phrase>, natural blinks, a small head turn, restrained jaw movement. After the last word <their> lips close and stay closed: <they> breathe and hold the lens.

    LEFT HALF, a new view every second, clean hard cuts: 0-1 s full body from the front, head to feet, feet on the floor; 1-2 s full body from the back; 2-3 s full body from the side; 3-4 s an extreme macro of <their eye> and <their skin>, real skin texture; 4-5 s <their hands> and <their feet>, with their costume details.

    Preserve exactly: <every detail on the sheet>. Use the attached authorized voice recording for <their> vocal performance. No music, no on-screen text, no outfit changes.
    ```
21. Quote the cost: the 480p draft at 3 credits a second (15 for 5 s), the 1080p finalize at 16 credits a second (80 for 5 s) ([[Flow v3.2 - 13 Services and costs]]). **GATE:** put the quote on a card that asks (a go, for `E-<CID>`) and wait for the user's go. Every job `count: 1`.
22. Submit the 480p draft.
    - Get the media ids of the sheet and the element line with `upload.py` (the upload flow: [[Flow v3.2 - 7 Takes]]): `upload.py plan $FILM <sheet> cast/<CID>-<name>/voice/element-line-5s.mp3`; call `media_upload` with the list it prints and save the response to a file without printing it; `upload.py put $FILM --response <that file>`; call `media_confirm` as printed; then `upload.py ids <sheet> <element line> --film $FILM --out $FILM/elements/<CID>/E-<CID>-take-medias-v<N>.json` for the `medias` list.
    - `generate_video` with `model: seedance_2_5`, `mode: omni_reference`, `aspect_ratio: 16:9`, `resolution: 480p`, `draft: true`, `duration: 5`, `generate_audio: true`, `count: 1`, `folder_id` (the film's cast folder, from `folders` in `$FILM/higgsfield.json`), the `prompt` (the prompt file's text), and `medias`: the locked head-to-toe sheet as `image_references`, the element line as `audio_references`.
    - The `higgsfield` MCP server cannot attach a video to an element (`manage_reference_elements create` takes images only), so the element is this clip, passed into scenes as a video reference.
23. When it lands, land it with `land.py --kind element --id <CID> --take <N>`, the result URL saved to a file (`--url-file`) and the job id (`--job`). It puts the clip at `elements/<CID>/takes/take<N>/E-<CID>-take<N>-480p.mp4` with `E-<CID>-take<N>.jobid`, and its card at the very top. The clip has landed when its file is in that take folder and plays in the Hitchcock Harness. Say in the thread what landed and on which page before any check, then run the checks below and write the findings into the card. Landing routine: [[Flow v3.2 - 7 Takes]].
24. **GATE: element clip.** Wait for the user's yes on each clip's card that asks, by ID (`E-<CID>-take<N>`).
    - A bad line in an element (a stutter, a missed word) is fixed with a redraft: a new 480p draft of the same prompt, on the user's go, landed as the next take.
25. **GATE: element finalize.** The 1080p finalize costs 80 credits an element. Once the clip is approved, put the quote on a card that asks (a go, for `E-<CID>-take<N>`) and wait for the user's go. Ask before any scene prompt needs the element, and inside 7 days of the draft.
    - Save the draft's params from `show_generation_by_ids` to a file in the take folder, then `finalize_params.py <params file> --draft-job $FILM/elements/<CID>/takes/take<N>/E-<CID>-take<N>.jobid --cost` for the free quote, and again without `--cost` for the job.
    - Submit `generate_video` with those params: the draft's full params, `draft` removed, `resolution: 1080p` and `draft_job_id`. It returns the same frames the user approved, at 1920×1080. Never a fresh 1080p generation: that is a new random take.
    - Land it with `land.py --kind pass --id <CID> --take <N>`: `E-<CID>-take<N>-1080p.mp4` and `E-<CID>-take<N>-1080p.jobid` beside the draft.
26. Write the element into `elements.json` under `elements.<CID>.video_element`, in the shape of `templates/elements.json`: `prompt`, `draft_job`, `draft_file`, `final_job`, `final_file`, `muted_file`.
27. A stray sound under the clip's silent end (a music sting): make a muted copy, `E-<CID>-take<N>-1080p-muted.mp4` beside the 1080p clip, and send that copy into scenes.

**Elements in scene prompts** (the prompts themselves: [[Flow v3.2 - 6 Scene prompts]])
28. Give every generation the 1080p element clip of every character in it (`final_file`, or `muted_file` when there is one) as `video_references`: a `VIDEO | <NAME> | <file> | @<NAME>` line in the generation's MEDIA.
29. `assemble.py` names each one as a numbered video character sheet, "[<NAME>] video character sheet, VIDEO REFERENCE n: ...". Its description, the `## <NAME>` section of `generations/prompt-common.md`, gives face, hair and costume in words.
30. End that section with the VOICE line: "VOICE: the voice heard in this reference, <pitch>, with a <accent> accent." Every line is spoken by the person named, in that person's voice from their reference.
31. End the TAGS of every generation that has a video reference with `@SHEETS ONLY`. It pulls the `## SHEETS ONLY` section of `generations/prompt-common.md`: "The video references are character sheets only: their split-screen layout, grey studio background and studio light are not part of this scene."
32. A character with a video element gets ONLY the element as its reference, in a first pass and in a redo: never put its sheet beside the element, the two compete. A sheet image (`IMAGE | <NAME> SHEET | <sheet> | @<NAME> SHEET`, described in its `## <NAME> SHEET` section) goes in only for what has no element: the extras' group image, a prop. The exceptions: a faceless sheet variant for a costume or prop state the element does not show ([[Flow v3.2 - 2 Characters]], sheet variants; [[Flow v3.2 - 8 Redos]]); the sheet of a character's second look (step 19); and the picture of a character beyond the three whose elements a generation carries (step 35).
33. Write the character, VOICE and SHEETS ONLY sections, and a sheet section for each extras' group or prop, once, in `generations/prompt-common.md` (from `templates/generation/prompt-common.md`). `@KEY` in a MEDIA description or a TAGS line of a generation's prompt spec pulls them in through `assemble.py $FILM <G> --version vN`; always pass the next version.
34. A voice without a body gets its element line as an audio reference (`AUDIO | <VOICE TAG> | cast/<CID>-<name>/voice/element-line-5s.mp3 | @<VOICE TAG>`, "AUDIO REFERENCE n"), and its section says "Every line <NAME> speaks is in exactly this voice."
35. Keep reference video at 30 s in total or less; a previz video is never fed. Over 30 s Seedance returns a 422 (not charged). At most three video references per generation, so at most three elements: the three characters who act most in it. When two tie, the third element goes to the one who speaks more in that scene: the element carries the voice with the face. Anyone beyond them gets a picture and a voice reference. The limits on references: [[Flow v3.2 - 6 Scene prompts]].

**Fixing a voice after a take** (the cut keeps each clip's own sound and fixes only the broken word: [[Flow v3.2 - 9 Edit]])
36. One wrong word: probe it first, with 4 second Seedance probes of the word said in various ways ([[Flow v3.2 - 8 Redos]]). The set of probes for one word is one quote and one go: quote the whole set once and make them all on that go. When the probes do not give it, fix it with a clone of the character's voice as the video made it, in many variations (below). Either way, the user gets the options as video, in the cut that contains the word, and chooses. The character's saved voice does not match the word as the video made it, even after EQ and pitch matching.
    1. Make a voice stem of the character's lines only, 25 s or more, from the keeper takes of that character: the `vocals` stem of a two-stem split by Demucs on the Mac, free (`tools/stems/split.py "$FILM" <take>`, into `audio/stems/<name>/`), cut to that character's lines.
    2. Make a voice from that stem: upload it (`upload.py`, `media_confirm` with `type: audio`) and call `create_voice_from_confirmed_audio`, named `<TITLE> – <name> (cloned from the video)`. It clones a generated voice. Never clone a real person's voice without their consent. Put the stem sample beside a sentence in the clone on a card on the Sound page, and say in the thread where it is: the user hears both before any word is spliced.
    3. Generate many variations of the word in the clone, said in different ways that fit the moment, with the sentence as context: `elevenlabs_v4` (`voice_type: element`, `batch_size` up to 4, `stability` from low to high) and `qwen_audio_tts` (`voice_type: element`, a short `instruction` per way of saying it). Ask for firmness and depth as "full voice, projected, resonant, firm", never as low or quiet: those make the voice whisper. Another way: `voice_change` on a short clip of the line, the clone as the target voice, and take the word from it.
    4. Give the user every option as video: each variation laid into the cut that contains the word, so the user sees and hears the sentence as it plays. **GATE:** put them on a card that asks (a pick) and wait for the user's pick by ID.
    5. Prefer a take that sits naturally at the character's pitch: shifting more than about a semitone by resampling changes the voice.
    6. Put in only the word, **dry**: no reverb, at most a very slight wetness (an echo at -30 dB under the word is too much). Put its vowel peak on the original vowel peak, where the mouth is open widest, so the lips sync. Match its loudness to the original vowel, with the clip's own background under it (the no-vocals stem, [[Flow v3.2 - 11 Sound and music]]).
    7. A generated clip's audio ends short of the picture and clicks at the cut: fade its last 60 ms and pad it to the picture's length.
    8. Transcribe the result with Whisper on the Mac (`tools/stt/transcribe.py --audio`): the right word reads at the wrong word's place.
37. Re-voice a whole character only when the user asks for it.
    1. Speak each of the character's lines in their saved voice on `elevenlabs_v4`, with the line before as the turn before it (step 10).
    2. Separate the old voice with a two-stem Demucs split as [[Flow v3.2 - 11 Sound and music]] says, and keep the clip's background (the no-vocals stem).
    3. Lay each new line in the old one's place. Mask the old voice over each line and its tail up to the next speaker, starting the mask before the old voice's onset so none of it leaks.
    4. Speed lines up with `atempo` where they must fit before a picture event.
    5. Match each line to the original voice: EQ to its long-term spectrum, a hall reverb matched to how much the original fills the gaps between words, and the level matched over the line.
    6. Put the scenes on a card that asks (a yes) on each scene's page. **GATE:** wait for the user's yes by generation ID before the lines go into the cut.

## Checks
- Every recorded take, by speech-to-text (Whisper on the Mac, `tools/stt/transcribe.py`, `tools/stt/README.md`): the words equal the line after folding numbers (`EQUIV`: "23" = "twenty-three"); no stray event ([clears throat]) unless the line is in `ALLOW_EVENTS`; a statement transcribed with a question mark is retaken. Retake ladder: the same tags twice, `stability: 1.0`, the first tag only, no tag.
- Pitch: measured for every option and every locked voice; a generated candidate on the candidate text, a preset on its own preview. A line under about 1 s: listen, do not trust the pitch. "The same voice as their other lines" means the same measured pitch and level (`measure_speech.py` lists the outliers per character).
- A voice saved from a sample: `list_voices` shows it ready before anything speaks in it; `voices/cast.json` holds its id with `voice_type: element`.
- Every line under 2 s has its padded copy (2.6 s), checked again after a speed-up or edit.
- The element line: cut from the picked candidate untouched (for a preset, from its one take of the line), no speed change, word for word, ending before the next word, pitch measured, the raw copy and the cut both present.
- The element clip, from full-size frames pulled by frame index:
  - the transcript (the Whisper route: `stt.json` and `transcript.txt` in the take folder) is word for word, and each word is said once: a stutter shows as unbroken sound with a stretched word before the repeat; compare the phrasing and breaths with the recording;
  - the pitch (`measure.py` on the `.wav` the transcription leaves beside the clip) lands within a few Hz of the element line;
  - every costume detail matches the sheet;
  - every view is there, in order, one a second on hard cuts;
  - the lips close after the line and stay closed;
  - no stray sound (music, a sting) anywhere, including the silent end.
  - Do not judge the backdrop: it can come out near white, and every scene prompt declares the split screen and studio not part of the scene.
- Every scene prompt: `@SHEETS ONLY` under TAGS and a VOICE line per speaking character; reference video 30 s or less (`check.py $FILM <G> --version vN` stops the submit; `--explain` says what each STOP means).
- A word fix: transcribed right at its place, no click at the clip's end, dry against the original.

## Outputs
- `voices/options/<CID>-<name>-<round>-<n>.mp3` (the preset previews and the generated candidates) and `voices/options.json`: per character the options (number, round, file, pitch, length, kind, voice type, voice id, job id, instruction), the rounds (brief and candidate text) and the chosen option.
- `voices/cast.json`: per character the name, voice id, voice type (`preset` or `element`), model, source and the user's pick note. Voices saved from a sample are on Higgsfield as `<TITLE> – <name>`.
- `cast/<CID>-<name>/voice/element-line-5s.mp3` and `element-line-5s-raw.mp3`, listed in `voices/element-lines-5s.json` (name, option, voice_id, voice_type, note, raw, file, seconds_raw, seconds, transcript, f0).
- `audio/raw/` (untrimmed takes), `audio/lines/<VID>-<name>.wav` (trimmed), `audio/takes/` (alternatives and replaced takes), `audio/padded/<VID>.mp3`, `audio/history/` (lines.json snapshots), `audio/table-read-vN.wav/.mp3`, and `audio/lines.json` with `chosen_take`, `note`, `alternates`, `previous`, `padded_file`.
- `elements/<CID>/E-<CID>-v<N>-5s.prompt.txt` and `E-<CID>-take-medias-v<N>.json`; one folder per take, `elements/<CID>/takes/take<N>/`: `E-<CID>-take<N>-480p.mp4`, `E-<CID>-take<N>.jobid`, `stt.json`, `transcript.txt`, the finalize `E-<CID>-take<N>-1080p.mp4` with `E-<CID>-take<N>-1080p.jobid`, and `E-<CID>-take<N>-1080p-muted.mp4` when a muted copy is needed.
- `elements.json`: per character, `video_element` with the prompt, the draft's job id and file, the 1080p job id and file, the muted file.
- `generations/upload-registry.json`: the face, the sheet, the element line, the voice samples and the clips with their media ids (written by `upload.py`).
- `spend.md`: a row per paid voice job (voices from the face, variants, saved voices, spoken lines), per element draft and per finalize, in the columns of `templates/spend.md`.
- Cards in the app: each round's options with kind and pitch; each element line on its own; each element draft at the top, with the check findings.
- A word fix: the clone voice, its variations and the video options made from them, the patched voice-only file for the edit.
- The Voices record: at release every voice in the film goes into `film-records/voices.md` at the studio root, one row per voice from `templates/records/voices-row.md` ([[Flow v3.2 - 12 Release]]). A listed voice is in use: never delete a voice without the user's yes on that exact name, after the deletion check in the standing rules.

## Tools
`S=tools/voices`, `T=tools`, run from the studio folder; `$FILM` the full path to the film folder. Run the voice scripts as `FILM=$FILM .venv/bin/python $S/<script>`. They call no service: every paid voice job is a Higgsfield call made through the MCP server, and the scripts handle the files before and after. Untested in this studio: `hf_candidates.py`, `pick.py` with `--voice-type`, `element_line.py` on Whisper word times, `record.py`, `takes.py --add`; check their first results against the checks above. Templates: `templates/` (`elements.json`, `higgsfield.json`, `generation/prompt-common.md`, `spend.md`, `records/voices-row.md`).
