# 5 · Previz and storyboards

**Purpose.** Turn the locked script into blocking and a designed camera: a scene file with its shot list per generation, each place built and checked before its scenes are staged in it, a grey Blender previz of every generation on the previz kit with its shots file, storyboard pages cut from it, a QC report and a continuity sheet per generation, an empty view behind every wide, and the whole-film previz and storyboard pdf the user checks.

## Inputs
- The locked script `$FILM/script.md`, cut into generations S1, S2, …, and `$FILM/brief.md` with the ratio, the film's code, the cast and each character's height and build ([[Flow v3.3 - 1 Setup and story]]).
- The picked location and its room: the room plan json `$FILM/previz/rooms/L0N-<name>.json`, already built from the picked picture and fitted to it at the location stage, the single source of where everything is in the place. Stage every scene of the location in it; do not build a second room. The props as they are approved and the views as they are made ([[Flow v3.3 - 4 Locations and props]]).
- Start on the picked location with grey stand-ins at the heights in the brief. Do not wait for the faces, the sheets or the video elements ([[Flow v3.3 - 2 Characters]], [[Flow v3.3 - 3 Voices and video elements]]).
- Standing rules: [[Flow v3.3 - 0 Standing rules]]

## Steps

### Scene text
1. Write one scene file per generation, `$FILM/generations/<G>/<G>-scene.md`, starting from `templates/generation/scene.md` and in its order: the header lines (location, length, shots, characters, lines, fight), the scene's conflict, the decisions, the geography with its compass (what lies north, south, east and west), a **START STATE**, an **END STATE**, the shots, the lines, the acting tasks, the fight beats, the storyboard beats, the notes for the previz builder.
   - Write the geography, the marks and every Builder line in the room's coordinates (its origin, +X east, +Y north, in metres), so the scene stands where the room has the doors, steps and seats. A scene file written before its room exists is refitted to the room once it does.
   - Length: the lines at each speaker's pace plus about 1 s a shot, in whole seconds, 4 to 30.
   - Build the conflict (goal, obstacle, tactic, reversal, value shift) with [[Flow v3.3 - 6a Prompt engine]]. Read it again whenever a scene does not work.
   - Write an acting task for every character in frame, listeners included, with [[Flow v3.3 - 6b Acting]] and the face rule in [[Flow v3.3 - 6 Scene prompts]].
   - For any fight, hold, throw or weapon, take the beats from [[Flow v3.3 - 6c Fight scene]]. A generation without a fight deletes `## FIGHT BEATS`.
   - Keep the three headings `assemble.py` copies into the prompt exactly as the template spells them: `## Shots`, `## ACTING TASKS`, `## FIGHT BEATS`. Every other section stays in the scene file for you and the builder. Under `## Shots`, the text before the first shot is not copied. Leave no `<…>` placeholder in the three copied sections: it would reach the prompt, and `check.py` catches only the short ones.
2. Write every shot in the Nexus shape:
   - a header line `### SHOT <n>: <SHOT NAME> (CAM FACING: <N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W or NW>)`, no seconds in it; `assemble.py` adds the panel numbers;
   - Size, Placement, Angle, Lens, Mount, Focus, a line each;
   - "In shot:" every character and prop in frame by its [TAG], and what each does, in order. No seconds anywhere.
   - under the shot, the camera's position in metres and compass, relative to the set or to something in frame, on its own line in single-asterisk italics, starting `*Builder:`. It is for Blender only; `assemble.py` drops every line that starts `*Builder`, and a bold Builder line would reach the prompt and stop `check.py`.
   - The Builder line keeps only what numbers cannot say. The kit writes its own line under it, `*Previz numbers (kit, <time>): …*`, with the size, lens, camera place, who is in frame and the look directions measured in the built scene (`precheck.py --write`, step 11). That line is replaced on every run and never edited by hand.
   - Place the camera by landmarks: the Placement line says what the camera looks at and where it stands relative to the set and to what is in frame. Write every character move in camera terms: screen left or right, toward or away from the lens.
   - The other shot-writing rules are in [[Flow v3.3 - 6 Scene prompts]]. They apply here, because the prompt is assembled from this text.
3. Plan the shot list by these rules:
   - **Size by lens**, given as the diagonal field of view of the film's frame: extreme close-up 30°, close-up 47°, medium 63°, wide 84°. 84° only for wides, where faces are small in frame; a shot built on a face is never 84°. The prompt's OPTICS line uses the same degrees. The lens table in [[Flow v3.3 - 6d Anamorphic look]] is for still plates, not for shots.
   - **Design the camera, not only the blocking.** Plan the shot list by the camera-design standard below from the first draft of the scene file. Depart from it only where the action needs it, and write that reason in the scene file's notes for the previz builder.
   - **Dialogue plays in close-ups (47°) and tight mediums (63°).**
   - **Keep each shot simple: one person and one action a shot.** Break a dialogue moment into close and medium shots: the speaker, the thing spoken about, the one who answers, each in a shot of its own. Never build a moment as one wide that holds many people and several actions. The video model keeps faces, costumes and placement far better close than far.
   - **Two people who talk while standing apart get a closer shot each.** Each line plays on a closer shot of its speaker, and the reply on a closer shot of the other: shot and reverse shot. A wide that holds both at a distance while one of them speaks is used only for a very specific, important reason, written in one line in the scene file's notes for the previz builder.
   - **Wides only where an object or the whole party needs them**, or for the few images that need the whole place, such as the opening or the ending of a scene: brief, at most one a generation.
   - A character wide the story needs stays in the generation from the start, for continuity. Split it out only if it fails ([[Flow v3.3 - 8 Redos]]).
   - **In 9:16, two characters side by side are always a wide**: two people 1.8 m apart need about 2.6 m of frame width, so both come out full length and tiny. Stage them in depth: over a shoulder, or one in the foreground.
   - **Physical action is shot from the body's level**: the camera at the height of the body that acts or takes the blow.
   - **Camera height and angle change from shot to shot**: neighbouring shots change camera position, height and angle, not only size.
   - **Plan action fluid across beats and cuts from the start**: each beat leads into the next and each cut carries the movement on ([[Flow v3.3 - 6c Fight scene]] for the beats).
   - A line of about 8 s held in one shot loses a sentence. Plan it over two shots, cut on action inside the generation, and let the line cross the cut.
   - **Close-up continuity lock**: when a shot cuts to a close-up of the same person or the same object, the first shot ends locked in the pose the close-up opens on (head position, eyeline, hands). Write the lock on both sides of the cut.
   - Every wide names the location view behind it by its tag. A wide stands on its own view (step 14) and has the Placement line: "exactly [<LOCATION> WIDE], the same camera, lens, framing and look, with the people in it". The shot says the view shows the set EMPTY and who is where in it for this shot ("in this shot A sits in the chair at the table, B starts at the door"), or the empty view empties the frame.
4. Double check the scene files before any Blender work: every transition from generation to generation is right, each generation's END STATE matches the next generation's START STATE (where everyone is), and everything makes sense.
   - **The seam.** Before the scene agents start, name every join where the next generation or the next cut is the next moment in time, and the characters who are in the last frame of the one and the first frame of the other. Write each in the START STATE and END STATE it joins. The place's owner builds each join as a named pose before any scene is staged (step 5).
   - At such a join, every character in both stands in the same place in the 3D world, facing the same way, in the same posture, holding the same things, whatever the two cameras are. The previz holds this exactly. A take is judged by eye: it passes when each person looks to be in the same place and posture across the cut ([[Flow v3.3 - 7 Takes]]). A seam counts above most other faults when choosing what to fix.
   - **The timing.** Before any render, check each line's words against its shot's seconds, and each scene's end against the next scene's start.
   - Once the shot lists are written or changed, bring the Edit page's timeline up to date, as the app's edit recipe says ("The timeline from day one"): the planned shots then show there.

### Blender previz
5. Set up the previz on the kit, `tools/previz/kit/`. Read `tools/previz/README.md` in full before the first script: units, compass, the helpers, the modes and what each writes.
   - **Film config**, once per film: `$FILM/previz/film.json`, in the shape of `tools/previz/examples/minimal/film.json`. `title` the public title; `prefix` the film's code; `ratio` `9:16` or `16:9` as in `brief.md`; `fps` 24; `res` 540, the short side of the draft frame. `cast`: one entry per character, keyed by its shot tag, with `height` in metres. `locations`: one entry per location ID, `{"name": "<the place>", "room": "previz/rooms/L0N-<name>.json"}`. The ratio sets the frame, the lens maths, the page layout and the whole-film assembly. Add a character or a room here before a scene script uses it. Two optional keys feed `precheck.py` (step 11): `aliases`, the names the scene text uses for a tag (`{"his sister": "MAREN"}`), and `speech_wps`, the speaking pace in words a second (2.6 when left out).
   - **The place first**, once per location, before any scene of that location is staged. One builder, the place's owner, makes the location's shared file:
     - the location's people file, `$FILM/previz/rooms/<LID>-people.json`, built once: the principals at their heights on their marks, every crowd and standing group, the props that live there, the marks;
     - the shared script, `$FILM/previz/scenes/<LID>_common.py`: the props' stand-ins (step 6), the light and haze, and the helpers every scene there uses;
     - the joins: every join of step 4 in this place as one named pose in the shared script (who stands where, facing which way, in what posture, what each hand holds), which the earlier scene calls at its last second and the later one at 0.0; and a joins note in words, `$FILM/previz/rooms/<LID>-joins.md`, one section per join.
     A scene builder never writes any of these. The commands: "What a scene agent does now" in `tools/previz/README.md`.
   - **The place check**, on grey stills, before any scene is staged. The owner renders the place with the principals in place, seated and standing, from the front, the back and the side, and through every window or opening the script names, and renders every scene's cameras from its scene file against the approved props. One QC critic round grades the stills (step 21's critic, its report `$FILM/qc/place-<LID>-r<n>.json`): stand-ins that read as what they are, the props as approved, no body inside furniture, no stray shadow or speckle, what must be seen through an opening seen from the cameras that need it. The owner fixes the place's own misses. The misses in a scene file (a camera inside a prop, text that describes an older prop, framing that cannot work at the film's ratio, a join described differently in the two scene files) go to the builders of those scenes before they start.
     - Then render every painted view's plate with `--plate`, which writes the plate's camera beside it, and run `views_cams.py $FILM --write`: it writes each painted view's camera into `locations/views.md`, so `precheck.py` can tell when a shot stands off its view's plate.
   - **Then the scenes**: one builder per scene, the shared file frozen. Each builder writes only its own scene's files. A change to the shared file or to a join is never made by a scene's builder: it reports it to the main thread, which passes it to the place's owner, who makes it once; the main thread then tells the other scenes of the place. A scene rendered before a shared change is rendered again before its next check.
   - **Scene script**, one per generation: `$FILM/previz/scenes/<G>_<name>.py`, starting from `tools/previz/examples/minimal/g01_minimal.py`. Its first line reaches the kit in the studio's `tools/previz`, five folders up from `previz/scenes/`: `import os, sys; sys.path.insert(0, os.path.abspath(os.path.join(os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__)), '../../../../../tools/previz'))); from kit import *`. Then, in order: `setup('<G>', <the scene file's length in s>, title='<generation name>')`, which sends every output to `$FILM/previz/<G>/` (a pick-up passes `FIX-<name>`, [[Flow v3.3 - 8 Redos]]); `R = location('L0N')`, the location's shared file with its room and its people (`R = room('L0N')` only where a location has no people file); `figure('<TAG>')` for every character the people file does not hold; the props; the poses and moves from the START STATE to the END STATE; one `shot(...)` per shot (step 7); `run(...)` last (steps 8 and 16).
     - The scene script loads the location's shared file and stages only its own action. It builds no one the people file holds. It moves or removes a crowd figure by name only where a shot needs it. A script that does not want a shared object hides it; it never deletes it.
6. Keep it **grey and super simple, the lighting kept**:
   - `figure()` gives every character the same plain grey mannequin at its cast height; no colour, no costumes, no designed objects;
   - the mannequins have eyes: two dark eyes, a nose and a brow, so where a figure looks reads in every panel. The eyes are fixed in the head, so a look is the head's turn;
   - a broader or slighter body is `figure('<TAG>', build=(<wide>, <deep>))`, or `"build"` in the people file. Never scale a rig for it;
   - position, size, pose, camera, screen direction and the light sources stay: the room's lights come from its plan; add any other with the light helpers;
   - render with `--grey`: every surface one neutral grey, every light and glow white, in the same places at the same strength. Water and foam keep greys of their own: give them `grey('water')` and `grey('foam')`, or they take the ground's grey;
   - give designed objects simple stand-ins: boxes and cylinders (`prop_box`, `prop_cyl`, or plan furniture), with `glow` on a part that gives light. A screen inside the frame shows nothing, or grey;
   - keep the mannequins articulated. Poses matter; colour and design detail go, because the model copies them into the video.
   - **The build ladder.** Start at blocks. Never build fingers or a closed hand: the video model draws a natural hand round a held thing whatever the panel shows. When the shot is about a held prop, give that prop its real silhouette: one smooth piece, with its curve and the way it points. Go up a step only when a take proves it is needed. This rests on one small test so far: check it on your films.
7. Give every shot its own camera with its real lens: `shot('<n>', t0, t1, <degrees>, <camera position>, <point it frames>, in_frame=[...])`.
   - The id is the scene file's SHOT number, so the panels, the shots file and the prompt line up.
   - The degrees come from step 3. Place the camera from the shot's Builder line (metres; compass 0 east, 90 north).
   - `in_frame` lists every [TAG] the shot's text names (characters, props, set pieces), location views aside. `check.py` stops on a tag the list leaves out, and on an entry in the list the shot does not name.
   - A camera move goes in `move=dict(...)`, a handheld shot is `handheld=True`.
   - Leave `focus=` out for a face: the kit then focuses on the nearest face in frame turned toward the lens among the shot's `in_frame` figures, and follows it as it moves. Or name a figure, `focus='<TAG>'`.
   - Two people who talk while standing apart (step 3): place them so both closer shots work. Each camera stays on its side of the line between them, and each speaker's head is turned so the eyes look toward where the other stands.
   - Each shot is one cut at its own times. Use `shot_plan([...])` only when a camera returns in a later cut.
8. Key every move with its timing and pass it to `run(moves=[dict(who='<TAG>', what='<the move in camera terms>', t=[t0, t1])])`; pass every line to `run(lines=[dict(who='<TAG>', text='<the line, word for word from script.md>', t=[t0, t1])])`. Both are written to the previz json. The prompt's moves are read from that file, in order, never typed, and the seconds stay in the file ([[Flow v3.3 - 6 Scene prompts]]); two references that disagree leave the model a choice. `check.py` compares the prompt's lines with these word for word. Where the previz departs from the scene file, record it in `run(extra={'deviations': [...]})`, as in `templates/previz/previz-take.json`.
9. Key the close-up continuity lock: the wider shot's last pose is the close-up's first.
10. Pose a prop that links two points across a body (cable, rope, leash) with `cable(name, points)`: a tube through its points, its end on the `end` empty, with no physics. Check the straight line between its ends against every body, route the points around each body, and let it sag between them. A hand reaching a raised-arm figure's head comes from the front, under the forearm.
11. Render `--check` and `--mannequin` (it shows the principals only, not the crowd and standing groups), then run the previz checks below. `--check` ends `IN_FRAME CHECK ok`, or prints a WARN for a figure a camera sees that its `in_frame` leaves out or a listed figure it never sees: fix every WARN. Fix the previz before anything is made from it.
    - **The free checks, after every change** in a scene. Run the script with `--facts` (nothing rendered, seconds), or with the `--check`, `--panels` or take that is due anyway: each writes the scene's facts. Then run `precheck.py $FILM <G> --write`. It writes the kit's line into every shot of the scene file and the board's camera lines (step 2), then checks each shot: its size against the scene file's word, named faces and hands in use in frame and not covered, a face turned toward the lens that the shot does not ask for, screen sides across a cut and across the seam, held props within reach of the hand, no body inside furniture, each line's words against its seconds, the panels of a shot different enough to show its action, every screen left or right in the text and the captions, a shot off its view's plate, a take or boards older than a shared change, captions carried from an earlier version. Fix every warning, or say in the scene file why the shot wants it.
    - Expect 9 to 27 warnings a scene, many of them real. Sizes of seated figures read one step wider than they are, and its reading of left and right in the text is a heuristic: a claim it misreads is a false alarm, not a reason to rewrite good text.
12. Render the previz take (no mode flag) and look at it yourself. Show it on the Previz step as a card for the generation, as the app's show recipe says.
13. When a blocking change reaches the scene text, re-render the previz and the panels, then rebuild the prompt. The model follows the picture over the words, and a stale picture produces an invention.
    - **A change after a take exists is a new layout.** A scene's layout is how it is laid out in time: its shots, their order and lengths, and where each spoken line falls. It is one previz take and the storyboard version rendered from it. When a scene's shots or times change after a take of it has been generated, the change makes the scene's next layout (Layout 1, Layout 2…), and the earlier one is kept: render the next previz take and the next storyboard version, never over the earlier ones.
    - The boards card names the previz take its storyboard version was rendered from, since the storyboard version does not record it. Every take records the layout it was made from when it is submitted ([[Flow v3.3 - 7 Takes]]).
    - The layouts are chosen under the scene's Takes on the Edit page ([[Flow v3.3 - 9 Edit]], step 1).

### Wide views from empty plates
14. Give every wide shot a location view of its own. Two wides from the same camera, or from cameras a step apart with the same framing, share one. Render the previz through the wide's own camera with the figures and what they hold hidden (`--plate <shot id> <t>`), as a full-size colour plate, then paint the view over that plate as in [[Flow v3.3 - 4 Locations and props]]: the plate as Image 1, the picked picture as Image 2 for materials and light only, at the film's ratio. Then run `views_cams.py $FILM --write`, so the view's camera is in `locations/views.md` (step 5).
15. Measure the view against its plate as in [[Flow v3.3 - 4 Locations and props]] and check it, detail by detail, against the room and the story. Whatever the view gets wrong, the take copies.

### Storyboard pages
16. List the panels in the scene file's Storyboard beats table: each shot's opening frame, every beat of action inside it, and its last frame. One panel per beat. Put their times in the scene script as `run(panels=[('<shot id>', t, '<caption>'), ...])`, each caption naming every figure by its tag with the action, so the page tells the identical grey figures apart. Without a list the kit takes three panels per cut.
17. Render the panels with `--panels --grey --version vN`, the next version every time: without `--version` the kit writes `v1`, and `assemble.py` takes the highest version. The kit renders each panel through its own shot's camera with the timeline markers removed, as `--plate` does; never render a panel any other way, or Blender switches to the camera the timeline holds at that moment. The run writes the panels, `panels.json`, `board.json` and the shots file.
18. Check `board.json` (shape: `templates/previz/board.json`), then lay out the pages with `storyboard.py`: 3300 × 2550, **three panels a page** in one row (840 px each at 9:16), so a wide's placement reads.
    - header: project, generation, ratio and length (written by the kit; `run(header=...)` overrides);
    - page 1: a layout note, where everything is as seen from the camera, and that the grey figures are placeholders whose look is not used. Add it to `board.json` as `layout_note` by hand, then delete the file's `"generated_by": "previz kit"` line so the next `--panels` run keeps your edits. A new version carries the hand-edited board forward by itself: the layout note and header kept, each caption carried to the panel of the same shot and time and marked `carried_from` until it is written again from the new frame;
    - each panel: its number, its shot number, a camera line (facing, lens, height, mount, written from the camera; `shot(..., board=...)` overrides it) and a caption with the action. Number panels by shot, never by second; no clip time on a panel;
    - footer: `storyboard.py` prints the same line on every page: the figures are placeholders, follow each panel's composition, placement, angle, screen direction and action, every look comes from the references. A new shot number is a cut; the prompt's STORYBOARD section says so.
    - **The whole storyboard on the Edit timeline.** Once a scene's storyboard version is finished and its QC round has run, bring the Edit page's timeline up to date, as the app's edit recipe says ("The timeline from day one"). The timeline then shows every panel of that version, each as its own board at its own time in the scene, until the next panel, with the lines at the previz take's times. The Edit page is then the storyboard in time: the user reviews and corrects the film there, scene by scene, before any video is paid for.
    - The timeline shows only a storyboard version that is finished and checked, the same one the user's boards card asks about; never a folder still being made. Never bring the timeline up to date while a version is still being rendered.
    - The boards card and the Edit page show the same panels. Where they differ, that is a fault: fix it before the user is asked for a yes.
19. Keep panels plain: no arrows, no labels. Only when words cannot place someone does an annotation go on, and then on a photograph or on a panel inside a page, with the arrows placed from the previz camera's own projection (`--project <shot id> <t>`, with any extra point named in `run(points={...})`) or a grid, never by eye.
20. Hand over pages only. The previz video never goes to the model, because a video reference carries motion and look, not only staging. A panel never goes in on its own at full size, because a single full-size grey panel becomes the look of the whole clip. The pages go into the prompt with their "position only" line ([[Flow v3.3 - 6 Scene prompts]]).

### QC
21. **The QC critic runs by itself** as soon as a generation's previz take and storyboard pages are made and its free checks have run (step 11). Do not wait to be asked.
    - From round 2 on, run `qc_recheck.py $FILM <G>` first. It lists every miss an earlier round marked fixed, in `$FILM/qc/previz-<G>-fixed-misses.json`, and the critic reads that list before grading: a miss that is back keeps its id and is marked reopened. The app's QC recipe says this.
    - Spawn one background critic per generation and round ([[Flow v3.3 - 0 Standing rules]], Agents), briefed by the app's QC recipe (`recipes/qc-critic.md` in the app's folder; its full path is in your instructions): stage `previz`, target the generation (`S1`).
    - It reads the previz data (the shots file, the scene file's START and END STATE, the cameras in the take json), the panels and pages, and the plates. It grades them 1 to 10 against this note's Checks, the camera-design checks included, and names the exact fix per miss: the file, the field, the value.
    - It writes one report, `$FILM/qc/previz-<G>-r<n>.json`, and nothing else. Never write or edit a report by hand: a correction is a new round.
    - Apply the fixes yourself, without asking, re-render the previz and the panels (steps 12 and 17), and run the next round. The last step of every fix: write every caption again from the new frames, delete each `carried_from` mark, and run `precheck.py` again.
    - When a critic's fixes clash with each other or fail in the render, the eyeline side and the seam win. The builder renders the fix once and reports any departure with its numbers. Stop at 8.5 or above, or after three rounds, and show the user the result on the Previz step; under 8.5 after three, put the report beside it and it is the user's call. A fix that needs a paid generation is quoted and waits for its go.
    - The same critic runs on each scene prompt before the video gate ([[Flow v3.3 - 6 Scene prompts]]).

### The whole film
22. Run the continuity pass over every shot of the film at once (Checks).
23. **Write each generation's continuity sheet** before any generation is made from it, and before every redo and pick-up ([[Flow v3.3 - 8 Redos]]). The previz is the source of camera, blocking and screen direction; the sheet is written from it and from the pieces in play:
    - who stands where (the marks);
    - what is in frame and what must not be (people, props, set pieces);
    - what the previous generation's last frame shows and what the next generation's first frame needs, with every character in both placed at the seam (step 4);
    - costume sides (which shoulder a pin or a strap sits on);
    - the sun or the main light: its side and height.
    Write it to `$FILM/generations/<G>/<G>-continuity.md`, a pick-up's in its own folder. The generation's references are only the previz panels of its shots, the empty location views, the sheets and the prop pictures, never a frame of a take or of any video ([[Flow v3.3 - 6 Scene prompts]]).
24. Assemble the whole-film previz and the storyboard pdf with `assemble_film.py --generations S1,S2,…`, every generation in film order (there is no default list; a generation with no previz yet gets a 2 s card). Show both on the Previz step beside each generation's previz take.
25. **GATE:** put the whole-film previz `<PREFIX>-previz-film-vN.mp4` and the storyboard pdf `<PREFIX>-storyboards-vN.pdf` on a card that asks (a yes) on the Previz step, and wait for the user's yes, by those IDs. On a change, fix the scene text and the previz, re-render the panels and pages, assemble the next version and come back to this gate. With the yes, go on to [[Flow v3.3 - 6 Scene prompts]].

## Checks
- **Previz against the scene text**, every frame, shot by shot (`--check` frames on a `sheet.py` contact sheet): foreground, background, who is where, facing which way. Pass: every shot matches its text.
- **In frame**: `--check` ends `IN_FRAME CHECK ok`; the shot ids in `<G>-shots.json` are the scene file's SHOT numbers; every [TAG] a shot names is in its `in_frame`, location views aside, and every character in `in_frame` is named in the shot.
- **Previz against the location view each shot will be given**: the same people in frame, the same foreground and background (the heights of fences, tables, heads), the same props, the main figure at the same size and place. Read the side-by-side sheet yourself; a subagent's report does not count. Fix the previz first.
- **Poses from a free view**: front three-quarter, side, back and top, never only through the shot camera, which hides a broken pose. `--mannequin` gives the close and full views of the principals only; for the top, render a `free_cam` looking down on the figure with the kit's `still(camera, t, path)`. Pass: every joint bends the way a body can, elbows and knees toward the surface a figure braces against, hands flat on what they press.
- **Props across a body**: the line between a cable's ends clears every body, and the cable hangs.
- **Lenses**: every shot at 30°, 47°, 63° or 84° (`diag_fov` in the take json); no shot built on a face at 84°.
- **Camera design** (step 3), each graded by the QC critic, a departure passing only with its reason written in the scene file and an action that needs it:
  - every dialogue shot at 47° or 63°;
  - a spoken line on a wide where its speaker is small in the frame is a miss, unless the scene file's notes give the reason (step 3, two people apart);
  - at most one wide (84°) a generation, brief, and only where an object or the whole party needs it;
  - every physical action shot from the body's level: the camera height in the take json at the level of the body that acts or takes the blow;
  - neighbouring shots differ in camera position, height and angle, not only size (compare the cameras' position, height, yaw and pitch in the take json);
  - action fluid across beats and cuts: each cut carries the movement on, and the panels either side of it continue the same action.
- **Moves and lines**: every move in the scene text is in the previz json, in order; every line is there word for word.
- **Continuity lock**: the wider shot's last frame matches the close-up's first (head, eyeline, hands), and the panels either side of the cut show the same pose.
- **The seam** (step 4), at every join named there, before a generation's previz is called done: its first panel beside the last panel of the generation or cut before. Pass: every character in both stands in the same place in the 3D world, facing the same way, in the same posture, holding the same things. A broken seam is fixed ahead of most other faults.
- **Every panel beside its previz frame at the same time** (the take's frame `frames-take-N/fNNNN.png`, NNNN = 24 × t + 1, or a `--check` frame), before the pages go anywhere: the same camera, foreground, background, characters and props.
- **Every panel's caption and camera line against the scene text**, shot by shot: the caption names every figure the panel shows by its tag, with the action the shot's "In shot" line gives at that beat, in order, never ahead of its panel; every character the shot lists in frame is in the panel; the camera line's facing, lens and height agree with the shot's header, Angle, Lens and Mount lines. A disagreement is fixed in the scene script or the scene text and the panels are re-rendered; a board fault found by the prompt check ([[Flow v3.3 - 6 Scene prompts]]) comes back here the same way.
- **Pages**: every panel number has a caption and every panel's shot has a camera line; no arrows or labels unless step 19 applies; three panels a page.
- **Continuity pass**, every shot of the film at once: each character at their story position; the same size and seating in every shot; furniture only where people use it, and a vacated chair where someone stood up; roles where they belong; any crowd the story needs is there; a front-only accessory is not seen from behind; every eyeline decided from the story moment and written, never inherited by accident; in a conversation between two places, each speaker's close-up looks straight into the lens.
- **Look at every render yourself** before handing it on. After any change to the kit, run the minimal example on a scratch film folder (Tools) and look at its check frames.

## Outputs
- Scene text: `$FILM/generations/<G>/<G>-scene.md`.
- Film config: `$FILM/previz/film.json`. Scene script: `$FILM/previz/scenes/<G>_<name>.py`.
- The place, per location: the people file `$FILM/previz/rooms/<LID>-people.json`, the shared script `$FILM/previz/scenes/<LID>_common.py` with the joins as named poses, the joins note `$FILM/previz/rooms/<LID>-joins.md`, the place check's report `$FILM/qc/place-<LID>-r<n>.json`.
- Facts: `$FILM/previz/<G>/<G>-facts.json`, written by the kit on every `--facts`, `--check`, `--panels` and take run.
- Previz take: `$FILM/previz/<G>/<G>-previz-take-N.mp4`, its frames `frames-take-N/`, its json `<G>-previz-take-N.json` (shape: `templates/previz/previz-take.json`; per shot the lens, position, yaw, pitch, move and `in_frame`; the shot plan; the moves; the lines) and `<G>-previz.blend`. Never edit a take json by hand: change the script and render the next take.
- Shots file: `$FILM/generations/<G>/<G>-shots.json` (shape: `templates/generation/shots.json`). The kit writes `duration` and each shot's `id`, `t0`, `t1` and `in_frame` on every take, `--panels` or `--shots-file` run, keeps every other key, and moves an id it no longer has to `retired_shots`.
- Check renders: `$FILM/previz/<G>/check/` (`shotNN-<id>-<t>.png`, `mq-<TAG>-<view>.png`).
- Plates: `$FILM/previz/<G>/plates/cam<id>-<t>-empty.png`; the wide view made from a plate is filed as in [[Flow v3.3 - 4 Locations and props]].
- Panels and pages: `$FILM/previz/<G>/storyboard/vN/` holding `panels/NN-shot<id>-<t>.png`, `panels.json`, `board.json`, `page-N.png`.
- The whole film: `$FILM/previz/film/<PREFIX>-previz-film-vN.mp4` with its json index, and the storyboard pdf `<PREFIX>-storyboards-vN.pdf`.
- QC reports: `$FILM/qc/previz-<G>-r<n>.json`, one per generation and round, and from round 2 on the list of earlier fixed misses, `$FILM/qc/previz-<G>-fixed-misses.json`.
- Continuity sheet: `$FILM/generations/<G>/<G>-continuity.md`.
- Cards on the Previz step, written as the app's show recipe says: each generation's previz take, the film previz and the pdf.

## Tools
Run every command from the studio folder (your working directory). `$FILM` is the full path to the film folder, `assets/films/<film>` under the studio folder; `T=tools`; `PY=.venv/bin/python`. Blender, the free 3D app, must be installed on this Mac (`/Applications/Blender.app`, or its program's path in `BLENDER_BIN`); if `blender_run.py` cannot start it, say so and stop. Blender runs only through the slot wrapper (the kit refuses any other headless run), one mode per run, `--res 540 --samples 16` until the user says otherwise. At most eight renders run at once on this Mac (the ceiling is `SLOTS` in `blender_run.py`); `SLOTS=1` in the environment serialises. The wrapper also holds a render back while the Mac has no memory for one more: the first four always start, and a further one waits and says `waiting for memory...`. That line is the wrapper working, not a hang: leave it to wait. In the scene-script rows, `...` stands for `$PY $T/previz/blender_run.py $FILM/previz/scenes/<G>_<name>.py -- --film $FILM --res 540 --samples 16`.
